A powerful storm flooded large parts of the river town of Easton on Friday night, forcing hundreds of families from their homes and cutting power to more than 12,000 households.

By Saturday morning, the river had risen nearly two meters above its normal level. Water covered the main street, and emergency crews used boats to reach residents trapped on upper floors.

The flooding did not come without warning. On Wednesday, the town council issued an evacuation notice for the streets closest to the river. Schools across the district closed on Thursday.

Not everyone left. "We have lived here for forty years, and the water has never come this high," said Ruth Alvarez, 71, who stayed in her house on River Road. "We thought the warning was the same as every year."

Town officials said about 300 people remained in the evacuation zone when the water arrived. Fire chief Daniel Brooks said his teams rescued 86 people by boat overnight, and no deaths had been reported.

Some residents blamed the council. "They built new houses on the flood plain five years ago," said shop owner Kevin Marsh. "Anyone could see this coming." A council spokesperson said the building plans had followed the rules in place at the time.

Scientists say storms like this one are becoming more common. A report published last year by the regional weather service found that heavy rainfall events in the area have increased by about a third since 1990, though it did not link any single storm to that trend.

On Sunday, power was restored to most homes, and the river began to fall. The council said it would review its warning system and the rules for building near the river.

Families returning home found mud on the floors and ruined furniture. The town has opened two centers where residents can apply for emergency help.

It is still not clear how much the damage will cost, or whether the new houses on the flood plain will be rebuilt.
